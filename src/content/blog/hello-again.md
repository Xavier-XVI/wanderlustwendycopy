It's been a while.

For years, "start blog again" sat on my new year's resolution list — right next to "drink more water" and "call people back faster." I would open a blank post, stare at the cursor, close the tab, and tell myself I'd try again tomorrow.

This year feels different. Maybe it's the approaching 40. Maybe it's that the twins are finally sleeping through the night and I have something resembling a functional brain again. Maybe I just got tired of my own excuses.

Either way: I'm back.

## Why I started this in the first place

I've been writing online since my early twenties — through Peace Corps in Cameroon, six years in Shanghai, a year and a half in Saigon, and a year-long around-the-world trip that still feels slightly unreal when I describe it out loud.

This has always been the place I process things. Not just travel — ideas, questions, frustrations, things I'm learning. A platform for sharing lessons, exploring what I think, and planning adventures out loud.

Motherhood created a long, necessary pause. When the twins arrived, I made a deliberate choice to keep them mostly offline. That decision still stands. But I've realized that parenting *is* one of the things I want to think through in public — carefully, without making my kids into content.

## The digital world is different now

I'll be honest: I've been watching the travel blog space morph into something I'm not sure I recognize. AI can now produce a "best restaurants in Lisbon" list faster than I can make coffee. Generic destination guides are everywhere, generated by tools, optimized for clicks, stripped of any actual personality.

I'm not interested in competing with that. I never was.

What I *am* interested in is the stuff AI can't replicate: the specificity of real experience. What it felt like to hike Ha Giang in the rain with a guide who spoke four languages. What it actually costs to raise bilingual children when half your family is on another continent. The uncomfortable, funny, occasionally mortifying realities of being Taiwanese-American and living *in* Taiwan as a semi-outsider.

That's what this blog is becoming.

## What to expect going forward

The new direction isn't a sharp pivot — it's more like an expansion. The travel content is still here and more is coming. But alongside it:

- **Worldschooling and education** — we're actively exploring alternative approaches for the twins, and I have a lot of thoughts and zero certainty
- **Raising multilingual, multicultural kids** — what this looks like in practice, not in theory
- **Family adventures** — travel changes completely when you do it with small children, and I want to document that honestly
- **Motherhood and personal growth** — the reflective stuff I used to write more of, but stopped

I'm not promising a posting schedule. I'm promising that when I publish something, it'll be because I actually have something to say.

## A note on the redesign

If you've been here before, you'll notice things look different. I spent some time rebuilding the site from scratch — cleaner, faster, mobile-friendly. The bones are the same. The voice is the same. Just less clutter.

If you're new: welcome. I'm glad you're here.

Leave a comment, send a message, suggest a topic. I genuinely read everything.

— Wendy
