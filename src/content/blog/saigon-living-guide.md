When I moved to Saigon in early 2019, I spent a lot of time asking people in Facebook groups for recommendations that someone should have already compiled somewhere. After a year and a half here, I'm that person. This is the list.

It's practical and opinionated. If you're moving to Ho Chi Minh City or newly arrived, bookmark this page.

## Facebook Groups Worth Joining

The expat Facebook group ecosystem in Saigon is genuinely useful — more so than in most cities I've lived in. The signal-to-noise ratio is better than you'd expect.

- **Fexpats – Female Expats and Locals in Ho Chi Minh City** — Strong, active community. Good for recommendations, safety questions, and general navigation.
- **Expats & Locals In Ho Chi Minh City (Saigon)** — The main general group. Large and useful.
- **Foodies in Saigon** — For restaurant recommendations, specifically.
- **Buy and Sell in HCMC (Sai Gon)** — Essential when you're furnishing an apartment or leaving and need to move things quickly.
- **Housing for Expats in Ho Chi Minh City** — Start here before committing to a neighborhood.

## Apps You Actually Need

**Google Maps** is more reliable in Saigon than in most Vietnamese cities for finding restaurants and cafés with real reviews. Use it.

**Grab** is non-negotiable. Ride-sharing, food delivery, and bike taxi all in one. It accepts US credit cards, which matters if you don't have a local bank account yet. Download it before you land.

**Now** (now called ShopeeFood) has a wider restaurant selection than Grab Food for delivery, particularly for local spots.

**Chopp** is the grocery delivery service that actually works — they shop for you and deliver within a few hours. Useful for the first weeks before you've found your regular markets.

**BTaskee** is an on-demand cleaning service. Affordable, reliable, and genuinely saves time.

**Lazada** is the main online shopping platform. Accepts international credit cards. Shipping is fast and the selection is broad.

**Payoo** handles utility bill payments — water, electricity, internet. Once you have a local bank account you won't need it, but it bridges the gap.

## Grocery Stores

**For everyday shopping:**
- Co.op Mart — the main local supermarket chain. Good prices, good variety.
- Big C — larger format, better for bulk shopping.

**For imported and specialty items:**
- Annam — the reliable expat grocery store. More expensive but consistent.
- Naman — good selection of organic and specialty items.
- Auchan D7 — French hypermarket, excellent wine section.
- MegaMart — Korean-run, strong on Asian imports.

**For specific needs:**
- Phuong Ha Store and Thai Hoa Grocery — Southeast Asian specialty items
- Ze Farm Box — organic vegetables, delivered

## Household and Practical Needs

- **Lock & Lock** and **Daiso** for kitchen basics and storage
- **Hachi Hachi** for Japanese household goods
- **Color Living** for home décor without the Annam prices
- **Sapuwa** for water delivery (essential — do not drink tap water)
- **Thien Hoa Electric** for appliances
- **Uncle Bills** is the expat hardware store of record
- **Gagaco** for plants (highly recommended — plants make apartments liveable)
- **Mi Store** for Xiaomi electronics
- **Saigon Photo Centre** for printing
- **MyStorage** if you're between apartments or need to store things

## Eco-Friendly Options

Saigon has a growing zero-waste community:
- **Nulla** — plastic-free cleaning products and household supplies
- **Lai Day Refill Station** — bring your own containers for refills
- **Limart Organic & Zero Waste** — organic food and sustainable products
- **A Little Bit** — sustainable lifestyle shop

## Gifts, Books, and Interesting Shops

- **OHQUAO Concept Store** — Vietnamese design, thoughtful gifts
- **Ginkgo T-Shirts** — the best Vietnamese-designed souvenir shirts
- **Artbook** — the best English-language bookshop in the city
- **Tranhart.com** — Vietnamese art prints

## Health Services

Finding reliable healthcare took me embarrassingly long to sort out. Save yourself the effort:

- **Maple Healthcare Chiropractic** — recommended if you're dealing with back issues from years of carrying bags through airports
- **Tam Duc Traditional Medical Clinic** — if you want to explore traditional Vietnamese medicine
- **Victoria Healthcare** — international standard, English-speaking staff
- **FV Hospital** — the main expat hospital. Expensive by Vietnamese standards but reliable
- **Westcoast International Dental Clinic** — good and reasonably priced by international standards

## Spa and Wellness

- **Noir Spa** — dining in the dark concept, worth the experience
- **L'Apothecaire** — French-Vietnamese spa, excellent
- **Spa La Sen** — local rates, high quality
- **Ace London Hair** — the only place I trusted with a cut
- **Marina's Skin Boutique** — for skincare treatments
- **Privé Nail** and **Top Nail** — both reliable for nail services

## Staying Informed

- **Vietnam Weekly** newsletter — the best English-language summary of what's happening in the country
- **Saigoneer** — English-language local news and culture coverage
- Various Vietnam Twitter lists if you want real-time news and commentary

This list will age. Things close, apps pivot, neighborhoods change. But as of mid-2020, this is the most useful collection of resources I know of for navigating daily life in Ho Chi Minh City.

If something's out of date or you have additions, leave a comment or send a message. This kind of resource only works if people help maintain it.
