We arrived in Sapa at 4am, stiff and slightly disoriented after an overnight bus from Ha Giang that took the mountain roads to avoid tolls. I would not recommend this bus. The roads are winding, the seats are not flat, and our fellow passengers were enthusiastic about talking at full volume until approximately 2am.

But we made it. And Sapa, once the sun came up, was worth it.

## Getting there

From Ha Giang, the most direct route is an overnight bus to Sapa town — roughly 8 hours, departing around 8pm and arriving around 4am. Alternatively, you can go via Hanoi and take the Chapa Express train from Hanoi to Lao Cai (2.5 hours), then a short transfer to Sapa town. The train option is significantly more comfortable.

We checked into **My Boutique Hotel Sapa** and slept until noon.

## Day 1: Sapa Town

Sapa town itself is a complicated place. It's grown fast — tourism built it quickly and without much planning — and the effects are visible. A lot of the development happened before COVID hit, and the shuttered businesses give it a slightly melancholy quality.

What I found harder to sit with: the ethnic minority children, mostly Hmong and Red Dao, selling bracelets and souvenirs on the main streets. Some were quite young. The sustainable tourism question here is genuinely difficult, and I don't think there are easy answers.

That said: the food is good, the views are stunning even from town, and it's a fine base. Just don't spend more than a day here — get out into the valley.

## Days 2–4: Trekking with Sapa O'Chau

The best decision we made in Sapa was booking a three-day trek with **Sapa O'Chau**, a social enterprise founded by a Hmong woman named Shu Tan that trains local guides and directs profits back to the community. Every guide we met spoke excellent English and had detailed knowledge of the land, the people, and the history.

**Day 2** covered roughly 14km through Muong Hoa Valley, passing through the villages of Lao Chai and Ta Van. The rice terraces were at peak green — we'd timed it well for late September. We stayed overnight with a local family in Ta Van, sleeping on thin mattresses on a wooden floor and eating the best tofu I've had in Vietnam.

**Day 3** was the hard one: 15km to Ban Ho, and it rained for most of it. The trails turned to mud. My boots, which I thought were waterproof, were not waterproof. Our guide, a young woman named Mai, was calm and patient and seemed genuinely amused by our suffering in the kindest possible way. I thought about her energy a lot afterward.

**Day 4** was a gentler 6km through Ban Den village before returning to Sapa. By this point we were grateful for the shorter distance.

**Cost:** $117 per person for three days, including guide, meals, and homestay accommodation. This is fair and I would pay it again without hesitation.

## Days 5–6: Sapa Clayhouse

After the trek, we needed recovery time, and we found it at **Sapa Clayhouse** — a small family-run mountain cottage about 3km outside of town.

The contrast with Sapa town was immediate. From the terrace, you could see the full sweep of the valley, unobstructed. There were no other guests the first night. We slept ten hours, ate homemade food, and read books.

If I were to do this trip again, I'd build more time here and less time in town. Two nights was almost enough; three would have been ideal.

**Cost:** $225 total for two nights (two people).

## Day 7: Return to Hanoi

The final morning was relaxed — coffee on the terrace, slow packing, a short walk. In the early afternoon we transferred to **Lao Cai station** (30 minutes from Sapa, about $3 by shared van) and boarded the overnight Chapa Express back to Hanoi.

**Train cost:** $35 per person in a soft sleeper cabin.

## What I'd do differently

- Skip the night bus from Ha Giang entirely — take the train from Hanoi instead
- Spend less time in Sapa town on Day 1; head out to the valley earlier
- Book a longer stay at Sapa Clayhouse
- The two-day/one-night trek option is genuinely sufficient for most people — most of the best scenery comes in the first two days

## Quick cost summary

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Three-day trek (Sapa O'Chau, per person) | $117 |
| Sapa Clayhouse (2 nights, for two) | $225 |

Sapa is not an undiscovered gem — it's a well-trodden destination with real tourism pressure. But if you spend your time with the right operators, get out of town, and put in the walking, it delivers completely.
